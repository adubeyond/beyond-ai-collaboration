# BEYOND 4.1.2: request cleanup and policy-refusal handling

Version 4.1.2 is a focused patch on 4.1.1, shipped as a complete package. It adjusts invocation and failure-handling guidance to reduce registration failures caused by bundled cleanup and prevent replay after cleanup fails. It does not alter platform safety policy or claim to eliminate every `blocked by policy` response. Earlier release assets remain unchanged.

## What changes

- **Separate registration from cleanup.** Request preparation, execution with result verification, and cleanup use distinct tool calls, not one shell, pipeline, or `finally`. Keep the request after refusal or an unconfirmed result. Ordinary deletion does not default to `-Force`; the patch does not remove that parameter from unrelated uses or bypass refusals when stronger actions require platform approval.
- **Classify the observed outcome.** Distinguish confirmed pre-start refusal, failure after execution starts, and an unknown result. A lost response calls for a focused readback of existing results, not blind replay of possible side effects. `never` means no approval prompt, not universal permission or proof of automatic review. Full access and administrator rights do not override explicit platform prohibitions.
- **Keep cleanup separate from transaction success.** Preserve and report residues without repeating committed registration or notifications. Workers report cleanup residues before returning, preserve the frozen final, and keep the existing final-notification order. PMs judge the managed transaction by its result, not by temporary-file removal, and do not repeat acceptance, acknowledgement, or dispatch because cleanup failed.

## Compatibility and installation

No runtime, formal Worker callback, model-policy, or retained CLI implementation changes are included. Existing sessions, results, reviews, engineering documents, and work are not migrated or rebuilt. Publication installs nothing into projects and resumes no tasks. The complete package still contains seven Skills.

Installation requires target-project authorization and a safe maintenance window. Preserve exact before-images and follow existing entry-fusion and integrity checks. Replacing rules on disk does not prove that existing conversations have reread them; do not restart or reconstruct active CLI sessions without authorization. ## Evidence and limits

New isolated cases use the real local runtime, receipt store, and workbench while injecting policy refusals and lost tool responses. They cover request retention, cleanup failure after commit, invalid runtime input, and readback of committed results without replay. They do not run or certify Codex's policy engine, establish every historical refusal's cause, or prove real-model compliance. A generic refusal with no explanation remains unknown rather than automatically attributed to a deletion parameter or user permissions.
